# Open questions

*The parts of this point of view that are genuinely unresolved. These are not rhetorical. A strong
strategy needs a defensible answer to each, and reasonable people will disagree.*

## 1. Does orchestration accrue to the rails or to the workflow?

Two credible outcomes. Either the participant that owns the rails and the balance sheet pulls the
orchestration layer toward itself, because funding and settlement are hard to replicate, or the
participant embedded in the customer's system of record owns it, because it shapes the decision before
a payments provider is chosen. The answer probably differs by segment and by execution speed. Which
segments are defensible for an infrastructure-led player, and which become pure infrastructure behind
a platform?

## 2. What happens to instrument economics, and how fast?

Rebate and float economics fund a large share of today's commercial-payments margin. As real-time
rails scale and carry structured data, does the instrument stay the margin engine for another
seven to ten years, or is the shift faster than it looks? A strategy that assumes permanence is
fragile; one that assumes rapid collapse is probably early. What does the honest five-year curve look
like under each assumption?

## 3. Where does liability sit when software moves money?

When an agent makes a bad payment, wrong counterparty, duplicate, out-of-policy amount, compromised
mandate, who is liable: the customer who granted the mandate, the platform that hosted the agent, the
model provider, or the settlement provider? Which participant is *structurally* best placed to absorb
and price that risk, and does being that participant create a durable position or an uncapped
exposure? The emerging standards do not settle this yet.

## 4. Will the supplier side pay for two-sided value?

Two-sided value is easy to assert and hard to price. What will suppliers actually pay for at scale:
faster settlement, guaranteed remittance data, financing, dispute reduction? How much buyer-side
economics has to be given up to fund it, and is the net still attractive? Is there a supplier segment
where this lands first?

## 5. How much of treasury genuinely requires a charter?

Balances, sweeps, liquidity, forecasting, FX: how much of a credible treasury offer needs an owned
bank charter, and how much can be delivered well through banking-as-a-service? Where is the line
between "treasury deepens the relationship" and "treasury is a distraction from money movement"? What
is the minimum viable treasury capability a payments customer would switch for?

## 6. What data-rights model keeps trust intact?

First-party payment data is the strongest underwriting input available, but using it for financing
needs a consent model that customers accept and regulators bless. Explicit opt-in per use?
Value-share back to the customer? Strict purpose limitation? Where is the line past which data use
erodes the trust that produced the data?

## 7. Closed or open, and can one strategy serve both?

If the future is a single financial operating system, owning the loop is the prize. If it is a
permanent division of the workflow across banks, networks, platforms and orchestrators, the prize is
being the most trusted interoperable layer. A serious strategy should be robust to both. Is that
actually possible, or does building for one foreclose the other?

## 8. Sequencing under a real budget

The horizons compound, but capital is finite. If Horizon 0 is genuinely prerequisite, how is it
funded without starving the Horizon 1 products that produce near-term growth? Is there a version where
a thin slice of the new core ships alongside one lighthouse Horizon 1 product, proving the model
before the full investment? What is the smallest credible first increment?
